Remote hiring call script

You are calling companies that posted a remote-friendly role and are still hiring. Your job on this call is simple: confirm the role is open, get to the person who actually owns the hire, and show them we can fill it with vetted talent for a lot less than they have it posted at.

Say the lines like a person, not a pitch. Get their number before you give yours. Never pitch someone who cannot say yes.

01Open and confirm the role is live
Light and quick, like you are just checking. You are not selling yet.
"Hi, this is Madison. Quick question, are you still hiring for the [role] position?"
Still open"Great." Go straight to who owns the hire.
Already filled"Got it. Do you hire for roles like that pretty often? I may be able to help down the line." Note it, be friendly, move on.
"Who's this?""It's Madison, I came across your [role] posting. Are you still looking to fill it?"
02Get to the person who owns the hire
Do this before you pitch anything. If they cannot say yes, you are wasting the pitch.
"Perfect. Are you the one handling the hiring for that role, or is that someone else?"
It's themKeep going to beat 3.
Someone else"No problem, who would be the best person to talk to about it?" Get a name. Ask to be put through, or for the best time and number to reach them. Even if you do not get through, you now have a name for the follow-up.
Gatekeeper"Totally get it. It is a two minute question only the person doing the hiring can really answer. Is the owner around, or when is a good time to catch them?"
Why: the whole call bends on this. Pitching the wrong person burns the lead and your time. A name is a win even when the call ends here.
03Who we are
Short. You are earning the next 20 seconds, not giving the whole story.
"So the reason I'm reaching out, I'm with Staffify. We fill remote roles just like this one with vetted talent, and our people sound like they are right here in the States. We've done it for over a thousand companies."
Why: the "sounds like they are in the US" line and the thousand-companies proof do the heavy lifting. Say it and stop. Do not monologue.
04The number
Get their rate first, then position against it. This should feel like a normal question and it should come up early, not at the end.
"What rate are you hiring that role at right now?"
React to their number, every time
"Oh, wow. That's quite a bit."
Whatever they say, high or low, you react the same way. It quietly frames what they are already spending as a lot, before you ever drop your number. Let it land for a beat, then position.
Then position against it
"Most of our clients end up filling the exact role they are advertising for 30 to 60 percent less than what they have it posted at. Our average client pays us about $12.90 an hour, all in."
They push on priceTie it to the role, not the hour. "For about what one local hire costs you, you could staff the whole function. Same work, different labor market."
Why: the 30 to 60 percent only lands once you know what they are paying, so ask first. The real cost driver is the labor market, not cutting corners, and that is the honest reason the number works.
05Name the overseas question, then ask
Most people are already thinking it. Say it before they do, then go for the next step.
"I know hiring overseas makes some people a little nervous. That is exactly why we vet hard, you only meet the top few out of hundreds, and we manage the placement. If someone is ever not the right fit, we replace them free, for life."
The ask
"If I lined up someone who could do that role at that rate, would it be worth 15 minutes to meet them?"
Why: naming the fear first takes the air out of it, and the lifetime replacement guarantee is your strongest proof you stand behind the placement. Leave the call with a booked time and the best email and cell.
Objections, fast
"We hire in-house / US only."
"Makes sense, a lot of our clients felt the same way until they saw the work. Would you be open to meeting one candidate, no commitment?"
"How's the quality?"
"We screen 150 to 250 people per role and you only meet the top 3, after a paid skills test on real work. Attrition is under 8 percent, and if anyone is ever off, we replace them free, for life."
"That sounds too good to be true."
"Fair. The reason the number works is the labor market, not corner cutting. Same work, and we back every placement with a lifetime replacement guarantee."
"Just send me info."
"Happy to. I'll text you a one-pager right now while we are on, what is the best cell? And while you have it, what is the first thing a great hire would take off your plate?"
"We are not hiring anymore."
"No worries at all. When you are, roles like this one are exactly what we fill. Mind if I check back in a few weeks?"
Rules, every call
  • Confirm the role is actually open before anything else.
  • Find the person who owns the hire before you pitch. Wrong person, get a name and move on.
  • Get their advertised rate before you give a number.
  • Sell the system and the lifetime replacement guarantee, not an hourly seat.
  • Give one honest reason to move now. No pressure, no fake scarcity.
  • End every call with a booked time or a named person to chase.
How to train on it
Run it, then listen back

Read the five beats out loud until they sound like you, not like a page. Then make live calls and pull your recordings in Outreach Proof to hear where you rushed, where you pitched too early, and where you forgot to ask who owns the hire.

Before every call, get the Dialer and this script open side by side. The moment a call goes well, log it and book the meeting on the spot.
