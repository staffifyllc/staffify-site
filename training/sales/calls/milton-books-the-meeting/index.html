Milton books the meeting

A Hiring now call to the owner of a California law firm who posted a Legal Assistant role at $25 to $30 an hour. Twice the owner tries to push the call to later. Milton stays friendly, makes the ask smaller each time, and books a Discovery call with Paul for Monday. Three and a half minutes.

Result: meeting booked

Listen for these moments (tap a time to jump)
The opener: who he is, then why he is calling
"I'm calling because I saw that you're looking to hire a legal assistant."
The posting is the reason for the call, so within ten seconds the owner knows why a stranger is on the phone.
One sentence on what we do
"We help companies hire dedicated legal assistants from Latin America and the Philippines, and we handle the recruiting, the vetting and the ongoing management."
One line, about their exact role. No pitch deck.
He asks for the meeting, not the sale
"Would you be open to a quick conversation with our team, so we can learn more about the role and see if we can put a few great candidates in front of you?"
The only thing he sells on this call is a short meeting.
Brush-off one: "call me later," and the first push
Owner: "Can you call me around 3:30? I'm not in my office." Milton: "How about this? How about you talk to my CEO? Not today, but I can book you for next week."
He doesn't accept "call me later." He offers something better (the founder) at a time that costs the owner nothing today.
The number, right when the owner resists
"I see your ad says $25 to $30 an hour. We can get you someone with experience for close to 13 bucks an hour."
The reason to say yes arrives exactly when the owner is looking for a reason to say no, and it comes from their own posting. Our standard line is $14 an hour.
Brush-off two: "I don't have my calendar," and the close
Milton: "I'm not trying to pressure you. I'll book you, send you an email, and you reply with your schedule if it doesn't work. How about Monday? Does Monday work for you?" Owner: "Yeah, that's fine."
He takes the work off the owner (reply to change it), then asks a yes-or-no question with one specific day.
He locks it down
He checks the time zone (Pacific, with the clock change that week), offers an exact time ("to give you some time, 11:30") and spells the email back.
A meeting without a confirmed time and email is not a meeting.
Rapport, then out
He switches to Spanish to confirm the details, says "I know you're busy," and wraps up.
Respect their time once you have the yes.
Why it works
  • Three asks, never an argument. Each time the owner pushed back, Milton made the ask smaller instead of repeating it.
  • He sold the meeting, not the placement. Nothing about contracts or terms, just a time on the calendar.
  • The money came from their own posting. Their rate, then ours, at the moment they hesitated.
  • He made yes easy. One day, one time, and "reply if it doesn't work."
  • He confirmed everything before hanging up. Time zone, exact time, email.
Use it on your next call

"Call me later": "How about this? Let me book you with Paul for next week instead."

"I don't have my calendar": "I'll send you an invite. Just reply if the time doesn't work. Does Monday work for you?"

The number: "I see you have it listed at [their pay]. We can fill it with someone great for $14 an hour."

Before you hang up: time zone, exact time, and their email spelled back.
