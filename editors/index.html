How It Works

How we find the right editor for your business

We don't just send you a portfolio and call it done. Every editor we place runs through the same rigorous process so you know exactly what you're getting, what it costs per video, and what to expect on turnaround.

STEP 01

We analyze your current workflow

Before we touch a candidate, we look at how video editing actually moves through your business today. What's your project management system? Where does footage live? Who reviews? Where are the bottlenecks? That snapshot becomes the spec we hire against.

STEP 02

Test projects on your real footage

We take your actual footage and have candidate editors complete real test projects, not generic samples. You approve the work before we move them forward. If their cut doesn't match your brand voice on real content, they don't continue. Full stop.

STEP 03

Background checks on the approved ones

Approved editors run through a traditional recruiting layer: reference checks, hardware checks, internet speed checks, and reliability testing to confirm they can hit your minimum refresh window on deliverables. If they can't ship on time, they don't make the placement list.

Cost per deliverable, modeled upfront

We track how long each editor's test project actually took, so by the time we recommend someone, you know not just how they perform, but the real cost-per-finished-video. No surprises after you sign. You see the unit economics before you commit.

Let's Do This!

Match your editor to the work you publish

A usable editing brief

Provide authorized footage, a reference cut, target length, aspect ratio, approved music, caption style, brand assets, and export settings. List the deadline and who gives consolidated feedback. Missing inputs should be flagged before the edit starts.

Capacity you can actually plan around

Separate simple cutdowns from multi-camera edits, motion graphics, and revision-heavy projects. Time a representative sample and include review time. A full-time editor has finite hours; adding outputs can mean changing scope or adding capacity.

A practical hiring and handoff checklist

  1. Choose a representative project rather than the easiest item in the backlog.
  2. Write a pass/fail checklist for technical delivery and a separate set of style notes.
  3. Review the sample for pacing, audio, captions, color, filenames, and exports. Record revision time.
  4. Agree on the daily queue, feedback owner, working hours, and what happens when an urgent request displaces another deadline.

Questions to settle before hiring

What kinds of video can a dedicated editor handle?

Roles can be scoped for real estate walkthroughs, social clips, talking-head videos, podcasts, or long-form content. Match the candidate to your actual footage, software, and output requirements through a sample task.

Is this unlimited video editing?

No. A dedicated editor works the agreed hours. Output depends on footage length, complexity, revisions, and turnaround requirements. The calculator on this page uses illustrative capacity assumptions, not a guaranteed number of videos.

How much does a dedicated editor cost?

At an illustrative $12.50 per hour and 40 hours per week, ongoing staffing averages about $2,167 per month using 52 weeks divided by 12 months. Onboarding is a small one-time investment. Your quote depends on the role and hours.

How do I compare an editor with a per-video service?

Compare the same deliverables, revision scope, turnaround, project-file access, and review time. A dedicated editor fits recurring work; a project service may fit occasional work. Use realistic capacity rather than assuming every video takes the same effort.
