DTC Customer Service Playbook: Scaling From 100 to 10,000 Orders

The staffing tiers, tools, and SLAs that hold from your first support hire to a full team handling 10,000 orders a month without breaking.

Staffify Team · July 29, 2026 · 8 min read

Every DTC founder hits the same wall. You cross 500 orders a month and the inbox stops closing. You cross 2,000 and Instagram DMs start piling up alongside Gorgias tickets. You cross 5,000 and your VA in the Philippines is asking for a raise, your response times are in the toilet, and your refund rate is climbing because customers are just charging back instead of waiting for a reply.

The problem is not that support is hard. It is that most DTC founders scale support reactively. They hire when the queue is already on fire. They pick tools because a friend uses them. They set SLAs after the bad reviews land. This playbook flips that. Here is the staffing model, the tools, and the SLAs that hold at each volume tier from 100 orders a month to 10,000.

Tier 1: 100 to 500 Orders a Month

At this stage, you are the CX team. That is correct and it should stay that way longer than you think. Founders who outsource support at 200 orders a month lose the single best feedback loop they have. You learn which product photos mislead. You learn which sizing charts are wrong. You learn which shipping carrier keeps losing packages in Texas.

Budget one to two hours a day. Batch tickets in the morning and again after lunch. If you are spending more than three hours a day on support at 300 orders a month, something is broken upstream. Probably shipping visibility or a product defect. Fix that before you hire.

Tools: Gmail with Shopify integration is enough. Do not buy Gorgias yet. You will pay for seats you do not need and features you will not use. A shared inbox in Front works if you already have one teammate helping.

SLA: First response within 12 business hours. Resolution within 24. Weekend replies are a bonus, not an expectation.

What breaks here: Founders who never build a macro library. Even at 100 orders a month, you have ten questions that repeat forever. Write them down. Save them as canned responses. This is the foundation of every scaling tier below.

Tier 2: 500 to 2,000 Orders a Month

This is where you make your first support hire. Not a full team. One person, full-time, dedicated. This is the highest leverage hire in your business right now, and most founders get it wrong by hiring too junior or treating it as a task-rabbit role.

What you actually need is an ecommerce CSR who has worked in Shopify before, understands the difference between a lost package and a stolen one, and can hold a friendly tone at 4pm on a Friday when the tenth customer has emailed about the same delayed shipment. This person will handle 60 to 100 tickets a day comfortably. Above 120 they start making mistakes.

You are also introducing Gorgias here, or Reamaze, or Zendesk if you enjoy pain. Gorgias is the DTC standard because it plugs into Shopify natively. Order lookups, refunds, and cancellations happen inside the ticket. That alone saves your CSR about 90 seconds per ticket, which at 80 tickets a day is two hours of reclaimed time.

Tools: Gorgias for tickets. Loop or Aftership for returns automation. Klaviyo for shipping notification emails so customers stop asking where their order is. That last one alone cuts ticket volume by 20 to 30 percent.

SLA: First response within 4 business hours. Resolution within 24. Social DMs within 6 hours.

What breaks here: Founders who hire one CSR and then take zero vacation, zero sick days, and zero training days from that person for six months. Build in coverage from day one. Even a part-time backup, ten hours a week, keeps the wheels on when your primary CSR is out.

Tier 3: 2,000 to 5,000 Orders a Month

You now need a team of two to three CSRs, and you need to think about coverage, not just capacity. This is where DTC brands start losing money quietly. Weekend queues pile up. Monday becomes a disaster. Response times slip from 4 hours to 18 and reviews start mentioning it.

The math on staffing at this tier: expect one CSR per 1,500 to 2,500 orders a month, depending on product complexity. Apparel with sizing questions runs hot. Consumables with subscription cancellations run hotter. Simple, low-return categories like candles or pet toys sit at the low end.

Coverage matters more than headcount. Two CSRs on staggered shifts covering Monday through Saturday will outperform three CSRs all working Monday to Friday nine to five. Your customer emails at 8pm on a Tuesday. Your customer emails at 11am on a Sunday. Somebody needs to be there, or at least respond fast Monday morning.

This is also where you introduce a team lead, even if informally. One of your CSRs owns quality, macros, and escalations. They spend 20 percent of their time coaching and 80 percent on tickets. You pay them 15 percent more. They save you 40 hours a month you were spending on QA.

Tools: Gorgias with automation rules turned on. Auto-tag tickets by intent (WISMO, return, sizing, damage). Auto-close order status inquiries with a tracking link. This alone deflects 25 to 40 percent of your ticket volume without a human touching them.

SLA: First response within 2 business hours. Resolution within 12. Social DMs within 4 hours. Weekend first response within 8 hours.

Tier 4: 5,000 to 10,000 Orders a Month

You are now running a real support operation. Four to six CSRs, one team lead, and someone (probably you or your COO) reviewing metrics weekly. This is where the wheels come off if you have not built the infrastructure in the tiers below.

The staffing model that holds: four to five full-time ecommerce CSRs handling front-line tickets, one senior CSR or team lead handling escalations and QA, and dedicated coverage for peak windows. Monday 9am to 1pm is usually the worst window for DTC. Post-promo weekends are worse. Staff to the peak, not the average, or your CSAT tanks in the moments that matter most.

At this volume, you also need to segment. VIP customers (top 5 percent by LTV) get a separate queue with a 1-hour SLA. Wholesale or B2B inquiries route to a different owner. Returns and exchanges get their own workflow because they have different economics and different timelines.

Ticket volume at 10,000 orders a month typically lands between 1,200 and 2,500 tickets. Contact rate under 15 percent means your product and ops are healthy. Above 25 percent and you have a problem that support cannot fix. Look at your shipping partner, your product quality, or your onsite copy.

Tools: Gorgias with full automation, self-service order lookup on your site, a proper help center with 30 to 50 articles covering your top FAQs, and shipping insurance (Route or Corso) to handle lost and damaged packages without human intervention. Add a chatbot only if you have the volume to justify it. Most brands do not until they hit 15,000 orders a month.

SLA: First response within 1 business hour. Resolution within 8. Social DMs within 2 hours. VIP tickets within 30 minutes.

The Hidden Cost of Getting Gorgias Staffing Wrong

Most DTC founders think about support cost as a percentage of revenue. That is the wrong lens. The right lens is contribution margin per ticket resolved.

A well-run support operation costs 1.5 to 3 percent of revenue. A poorly run one costs 4 to 7 percent, not because you are paying more, but because slow responses drive refunds, chargebacks, and lost repeat purchases. A customer who gets a reply in 2 hours has an 85 percent chance of buying again. A customer who waits 24 hours drops to 55 percent. A customer who waits 48 hours or gets a canned reply that misses their actual question basically never comes back.

The difference between good and bad support at 5,000 orders a month is roughly $40,000 to $60,000 in annual repeat revenue. That pays for a full-time senior CSR three times over.

The other hidden cost: founder time. If you are still touching tickets at 5,000 orders a month, you are spending 8 to 12 hours a week on work that a $22 an hour CSR can do better than you can. That is 400 to 600 hours a year. What could you build with 500 hours of focused founder time?

How to Hire Ecommerce CSRs Who Actually Stick

Hiring for support is not hiring for warm bodies. The best ecommerce CSRs share three traits. They write clearly. They handle emotional customers without absorbing the emotion. They notice patterns and flag them.

Screen with a written exercise. Send them three sample tickets: a WISMO, an angry refund request, and an ambiguous sizing question. Score the responses on tone, accuracy, and brevity. This one test filters out 70 percent of candidates in 20 minutes.

Pay above market for the role. A Shopify customer support hire at $18 an hour will churn in six months. The same hire at $24 an hour with a clear growth path stays two years and becomes your team lead. The math on retention is brutal. Every CSR you replace costs you roughly 400 hours of ramp time and a measurable dip in customer satisfaction while they learn.

Set clear metrics from day one. First response time, resolution time, CSAT, and tickets resolved per day. Review them weekly. Coach on the ones that slip. Do not wait for a quarterly review to tell someone their CSAT is at 78.
