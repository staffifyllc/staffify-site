VA Hiring Location Compared: Philippines vs LatAm vs US

An honest breakdown of cost, English, time zones, and talent depth across the three places most service businesses hire virtual assistants from.

Staffify Team · October 9, 2026 · 7 min read

Every founder I talk to asks the same question before their first VA hire: where should I look? The internet has strong opinions. Reddit swears by the Philippines. Twitter hypes LatAm. Your mastermind buddy insists you need someone domestic. The truth is messier. Each location is great for some roles and terrible for others, and the right answer depends on what you actually need done.

I've placed people from all three regions into service businesses doing $300K to $5M. Here's what actually matters when you pick a hiring location, with no sugarcoating.

Cost: What You'll Actually Pay

Let's get the money out of the way first, because it drives most decisions.

A full-time Philippines virtual assistant with 2 to 5 years of experience runs $1,200 to $2,200 per month fully loaded. Specialists like video editors or paid ads managers can hit $2,500 to $3,500. These are rates for strong, vetted talent, not the $400 Upwork gig that will ghost you in six weeks.

A LatAm VA from Mexico, Colombia, Argentina, or the Dominican Republic typically costs $1,800 to $3,500 full-time. Senior roles like executive assistants to a founder, sales development reps, or bookkeepers push $3,500 to $5,500. You pay a premium for the time zone and the Spanish-English fluency.

A US-based VA, if you can even find one who wants a VA role instead of a staff job, is $4,500 to $8,000 per month for the same work. Add benefits, payroll taxes, and the W-2 overhead if you employ them directly, and you're closer to $75K to $110K a year.

The math is obvious, but founders get it wrong anyway. They hire the cheapest option, lose three months to turnover, and conclude "offshore doesn't work." What didn't work was skipping the vetting. A $2,000 Filipino VA who stays two years beats a $600 one who lasts eight weeks, every time.

English Fluency and Communication

This is where the stereotypes break down fast.

Filipino VAs generally have the strongest written English of the offshore options. The Philippines has English as an official language, the education system runs in English, and the BPO industry has trained millions of people to communicate with American clients. For email, Slack, documentation, SOPs, and internal process work, a solid Filipino VA will be nearly indistinguishable from a US hire in writing.

Spoken English is more variable. Accent is fine for most internal work. For customer-facing phone calls, especially cold outbound, it can be a friction point. Not a dealbreaker, but something to test for the specific role.

LatAm VAs are more variable overall. The top 20% are fully bilingual, often having studied or worked in the US, and sound neutral on calls. The middle 60% have functional business English with a noticeable accent. The bottom 20% should not be doing English-first work, period. If you're hiring from LatAm, you need a vetting process that actually tests speaking, not just a resume review.

US-based VAs obviously win on native fluency, idiom, and cultural fit. The question is whether you need that for the specific role. For most back-office work, you don't.

Time Zone Reality

Time zone is the single most under-discussed factor in va hiring location decisions. Founders fixate on cost and miss that a 12-hour gap changes how the role actually functions.

Philippines is 12 to 15 hours ahead of US time zones. Most Filipino VAs working for US clients flip their schedule and work US business hours, which is standard and expected. They're used to it. The real question is whether that's what you want. For roles that benefit from an overnight handoff model, like a video editor who gets footage at 5pm EST and delivers a cut by 9am, the Philippines time zone is actually an advantage. For a sales assistant who needs to coordinate meetings in real time with your prospects, the schedule flip is fine but you lose the natural overlap with their local life, which can affect long-term retention.

LatAm is the clear winner for real-time collaboration. A VA in Mexico City or Bogotá is on CST or EST. You can jump on a call at 2pm without anyone sacrificing their evening. For roles that require constant back-and-forth with you or your team, like executive assistants, inside sales, or client success, this matters more than people realize. The difference between "ping me on Slack" and "wait 12 hours for an answer" compounds.

US-based talent is in your time zone. That's it. For some roles that's worth the 3x price tag. For most it isn't.

Work Culture and Retention

This is the stuff that doesn't show up in a comparison chart but determines whether your hire works out.

Filipino work culture is relationship-driven and loyalty-oriented. People who feel respected and secure tend to stay for years. The flip side: there's a cultural tendency to avoid delivering bad news or pushing back on a boss. If you want a VA who will tell you your SOP is broken or your idea won't work, you need to actively build that into the relationship. It won't come naturally in the first six months. Once you earn trust, you get it.

LatAm culture, especially in Mexico, Argentina, and Colombia, is more direct. People will push back, offer opinions, suggest changes. This is great if you want a thought partner. It's friction if you want someone to just execute. LatAm hires also tend to job-hop more aggressively, especially the strong ones, because the US remote market is actively poaching them. Expect to pay market rate or get replaced.

US-based VAs are a mixed bag. The ones who want VA work long-term often have specific life situations: caregivers, people in low cost-of-living areas, semi-retired professionals. They can be fantastic. But many US-based "VAs" are using the role as a stepping stone to something else and will leave within a year.

Talent Depth by Role Type

Here's where the location decision actually gets made. Different regions have deeper benches for different roles.

Video Editing and Content Production

The Philippines wins decisively. There's a massive, mature pool of editors working on YouTube, podcast, and short-form content for American creators. Rates are reasonable, turnaround is fast, and the time zone offset actually helps. LatAm has solid editors but fewer of them, and rates are 30 to 50% higher for comparable skill.

Executive Assistants

LatAm wins for founder-facing EA work. The time zone, the direct communication style, and the ability to jump on a quick call make a real difference when you need someone managing your calendar, inbox, and travel in real time. Philippines EAs are excellent for process-heavy EA work (scheduling, data entry, inbox triage) but less ideal when the role requires live judgment calls with you.

B2B Lead Generation and SDR Work

Depends on the channel. For email, LinkedIn, and research-based outbound, the Philippines has incredible depth and value. For cold calling US prospects, LatAm or US-based wins because accent and real-time response matter. Nobody wants their cold caller operating on a 14-hour delay.

Bookkeeping and Finance Ops

Philippines is strong here. There's a deep pool of QuickBooks and Xero certified bookkeepers who've worked with US small businesses for years. LatAm is catching up but shallower. US-based CPAs make sense only if you need actual tax work or audit prep.

Customer Support

Written support (email, chat): Philippines, all day. Phone support with US customers: LatAm or US-based, depending on your customer expectations.

Paid Ads and Marketing Ops

Both regions have strong talent. LatAm skews slightly more strategic, Philippines slightly more executional. If you need someone to run and optimize campaigns under your direction, Philippines is better value. If you want someone to own strategy and report to the board, LatAm or US.

How to Actually Decide

Stop asking "which location is best" and start asking three questions.

  1. Does this role require real-time collaboration with me or my team? If yes, LatAm. If no, Philippines.
  2. Does this role involve speaking with US customers on the phone? If yes, LatAm or US. If no, Philippines is fine.
  3. Is the role process-heavy execution or judgment-heavy ownership? Execution leans Philippines. Ownership leans LatAm or US.

Most service businesses between $300K and $5M end up with a blended team. A Filipino VA handling video editing, inbox management, and bookkeeping. A LatAm hire running sales ops or acting as EA. Maybe a US-based fractional specialist for a specific function. The founders who get stuck are the ones trying to find one person from one region to do everything.

The Vetting Problem Nobody Talks About

Here's the uncomfortable part. The location matters less than the hiring process. A great Filipino VA will outperform a mediocre US hire. A great LatAm VA will outperform a mediocre Filipino VA. And a bad hire from any region will cost you 90 days and your faith in the whole model.

Most founders don't have a real vetting process. They post on OnlineJobs.ph, interview three people in a week, pick the one who seems nicest, and hope. Then they're surprised when it doesn't work.

A real process looks like this. Define the role in writing with specific weekly outcomes. Screen for English fluency with a written task and a live call. Give a paid trial project that mirrors the actual work. Check references by phone, not email. Make an offer that pays market rate for the location, not the floor. Invest in onboarding for the first 30 days like you would for any key hire.

Do that in any of the three regions and you'll be fine. Skip it and no location will save you.

The best time to pick a hiring location is after you've written the job description and listed the three or four outcomes that matter most. Read those outcomes. The right region will usually be obvious. If it isn't, you probably haven't defined the role tightly enough yet, which is the real problem to solve first.
