7 VA Management Tools Every Service Business Actually Needs

A no-fluff stack for running remote virtual assistants without losing your mind, your passwords, or three hours a day to status updates.

Staffify Team · September 11, 2026 · 6 min read

The first time you hire a virtual assistant, you think the hard part is finding them. It isn't. The hard part starts on day 12, when you realize you have no idea what they did yesterday, they've asked the same question four times in three different apps, and you're still the only person who knows the Google Ads password.

Most operators try to fix this by hiring another VA. Wrong move. What you actually need is a small, boring stack of tools that turns your VA from a person you manage into a role that runs. Here are the seven we recommend to service businesses running one to fifteen remote hires.

1. Loom for asynchronous training

If you type out SOPs from scratch, you'll never finish. If you record Loom videos while you do the work, you'll have a training library in a month.

The rule we give operators: any task you're about to explain twice, record once. A five-minute Loom beats a two-page written SOP nine times out of ten, because the VA sees the actual clicks, the actual client account, the actual dashboard.

Two habits that make Loom pay off:

  • Name videos by the task, not the date. "Monthly client report build" beats "Loom 47."
  • Drop the link inside the SOP doc, not just in Slack. Slack messages disappear. Docs don't.

Free plan works fine to start. Once you have more than three people on your team, the $15/month Business plan is worth it for the transcript search alone.

2. ClickUp (or Asana) for the work itself

You need one place where every task lives. Not Slack. Not email. Not a Google Doc with 40 checkboxes.

We use ClickUp with clients because it handles both recurring work (weekly reports, monthly invoicing, daily lead lists) and project work (client onboarding, launch prep) in the same view. Asana works just as well if you already know it. The tool matters less than the discipline.

The discipline is this: if a task isn't in the system, it doesn't exist. No "hey can you quickly..." messages that turn into missed deliverables. Every request becomes a task with an owner, a due date, and a description. This feels like overkill for two weeks. Then it feels like the only reason your business runs.

Set up recurring tasks for anything your VA does more than twice. A B2B lead gen VA should have a task auto-generated every Monday morning: "Pull 100 net-new prospects, LinkedIn + Apollo, target list attached." They open ClickUp, see the task, do the work. No meeting required.

3. Slack for the conversation layer

Slack is not your task manager. Slack is not your file storage. Slack is where humans talk about the work happening in the other tools.

Keep it clean:

  • One channel per client or per function. Not both.
  • A #daily-standup channel where every VA posts what they did yesterday, what they're doing today, and what's blocking them. Three bullets, takes 90 seconds, replaces a meeting.
  • DMs are for private stuff only. Everything else goes in a channel so the team can search it later.

If your VA works in a different time zone (most do), Slack's scheduled send feature is a small but real upgrade. You draft the request at 11pm your time, it lands in their morning. No expectation-setting problems.

4. A shared password manager

This is the tool operators skip and then regret. If you're still sending passwords over Slack, iMessage, or (please no) email, stop reading and go fix that today.

1Password and Bitwarden both work. 1Password is more polished, Bitwarden is cheaper. Either one lets you share credentials with your VA without them ever seeing the actual password. When the VA leaves, you revoke access in one click. No changing 30 passwords, no wondering if they still have the login to your ad account.

Set up shared vaults by function: Marketing, Finance, Client Accounts, Internal Tools. Give each VA access only to what they need. This isn't paranoia. It's the difference between a clean offboarding and a Sunday night panic.

5. Screen recording for QA and feedback

Loom shows up twice on this list for a reason. Once you're using it to train, start using it to give feedback.

Written feedback on a deliverable takes forever and lands wrong. "Can you make the intro punchier?" means nothing. A 90-second Loom where you open the doc, highlight the paragraph, and say "cut these two sentences, start with the number, then bring back the setup" means everything.

Same goes for QA. Have your VA record a Loom walking through their work before they hand it off. A video editor showing you the timeline. An admin showing you the CRM update. A lead gen VA showing you the criteria they used to build the list. You catch problems in 60 seconds that would take 20 minutes of back-and-forth in Slack.

6. Time tracking that isn't creepy

Full-time VAs don't need surveillance software. They need clarity on where their hours go so you can price your services correctly and spot capacity issues before they become fires.

Toggl and Harvest are the two we recommend. Both let the VA click a timer, tag it to a client or project, and move on. You get a weekly report showing where 40 hours went. That's it.

What to do with the data:

  1. Every Friday, look at the report. Which client took twice the hours you quoted? That's your next pricing conversation.
  2. If your VA is spending 15 hours a week on one client, they're near capacity. Start planning the next hire before you're desperate.
  3. If tasks are consistently taking 40% longer than estimated, your SOPs need work or the scope is wrong.

Skip the ones that take screenshots every 10 minutes. They kill trust, and any decent VA will refuse to work under them.

7. A single dashboard for the metrics that matter

The last tool isn't really a tool. It's a discipline. You need one page you can look at every Monday morning that tells you if your remote team is winning or losing.

For most service businesses, that's a Google Sheet or a Notion page with maybe eight numbers on it. Not a fancy BI tool. Not a $200/month dashboard product. Just the metrics that matter to your business:

  • For a video editing team: videos delivered, revision cycles per video, average turnaround time.
  • For an admin team: inbox response time, meetings booked, tasks closed on time.
  • For a lead gen team: prospects added, replies received, meetings booked, reply rate.

Your VA updates it. You review it. If a number moves the wrong way for two weeks, you have a conversation. If it moves the right way, you know the system is working and you can go back to selling and serving clients instead of babysitting.

The stack costs less than one bad hire

Add it all up and you're looking at maybe $150 to $250 per month for a team of five. That's less than a single afternoon of the wrong hire redoing work.

The mistake we watch operators make is trying to buy their way out of a management problem with more tools. That's backwards. The tools above work because they force a small number of habits: record instead of explain, task instead of message, review instead of assume, measure instead of hope.

Pick the two you don't have yet. Set them up this week. Then hire the VA, or fix the one you already have, into a system that doesn't require you to be the smartest person in every conversation. That's when remote hires stop feeling like a gamble and start feeling like leverage.
