Dedicated real estate video editors

Build a repeatable handoff for listing tours, agent reels, and drone footage. Match the editor to your footage, brand, software, and delivery requirements.

By Staffify · Updated September 22, 2026

Quick answer

A dedicated editor works your agreed hours and scope. Turnaround and output depend on footage, complexity, revisions, and the queue. Test a representative listing before planning capacity around a fixed number of videos.

Choose the outputs before the editor

A listing tour, vertical teaser, agent introduction, and multi-camera interview are different jobs. Specify which you need and how often. Your candidate assessment should use those formats, rather than a generic highlight reel.

Listing walkthroughs

Define room order, property details, length, audio requirements, and export format. Include reference work that shows how you handle interiors, windows, exterior shots, and drone transitions.

Agent reels and social clips

Provide approved messaging, caption spelling, safe areas, branding, and the number of cutdowns required. Confirm whether the editor creates the cut from a brief or needs you to select the moments first.

A listing-video brief you can copy

  • Job: property identifier, client, project owner, deadline, and time zone.
  • Inputs: footage folder, approved voiceover, brand kit, reference cut, and music you are permitted to use.
  • Outputs: length, aspect ratio, resolution, frame rate, caption file or burned-in captions, and naming convention.
  • Required details: approved agent name, contact information, logo, and any required end card.
  • Review: one feedback owner, review link, revision priority, and who gives final approval.
  • Stop rules: missing footage, uncertain property facts, unclear usage rights, or a deadline the current scope cannot meet.

Review the sample consistently

Check audio levels and intelligibility, caption accuracy, transitions, shot order, color consistency, correct branding, and whether every promised export opens. Keep technical requirements separate from taste notes so a candidate can act on specific feedback.

Track edit time, revision time, and your own review time. Repeat this across a representative mix of projects before deciding how many weekly deliverables one editor can support.

Editing and delivery coordination are different roles

An editor makes the cut. A coordinator can check filenames, track feedback, prepare delivery links, and answer status questions. Your creative approval still needs a named owner. See the real estate media handoff guide for that division of responsibility and a starter checklist.

Compare dedicated and per-project options

A dedicated editor can fit steady recurring production and a consistent brand. A per-project service can fit occasional work. Compare the same footage, outputs, revisions, turnaround, project-file access, software costs, and management effort rather than price alone.

Frequently asked questions

What can a real estate video editor produce?

Depending on the agreed role, an editor can produce listing walkthroughs, agent reels, social cutdowns, drone sequences, and branded exports. Confirm software, footage requirements, and output formats with sample work.

How fast will listing videos be delivered?

Agree on turnaround after reviewing your footage, output requirements, revision process, and queue. A full-time placement does not guarantee the same deadline for every project.

Is a dedicated editor worthwhile for occasional listings?

It depends on recurring workload. Compare project-based editing with the cost and usable capacity of a dedicated role. A small or seasonal queue may not justify a full-time placement.
